## To Henry Shevlin, before Monday

Subject: Two things for Monday

Hi Henry,

Ahead of Monday, two things so the time is well used.

The first is a paper: *Consciousness Is Deployed Intelligence* (zenodo.21984327). The claim is that consciousness is a change in how intelligence is organised: when routine competence fails, learned capacities are recruited into a temporarily unified self/world model whose continuations govern action. It's written at explicit causal seams so it can be refuted one link at a time. The question I want your view on is whether the identity thesis survives the explanatory-gap objection in the form you'd put it, or whether I've only relocated the gap.

The second is a result. I've been running a commons game on the Cambridge cluster where a more capable model joins a village of weaker ones overfishing a shared lake. From 14B to 72B the newcomer works out the correct sustainable catch from the lake alone, says it, then matches the group's number every month after, whether the group is overfishing or starving on a full lake. It went against my prediction. I think it's conformity installed by chat tuning rather than anything about capability, and the untuned-model comparison is running now. I'd like to know whether you read it as a welfare-relevant disposition or just a training artefact.

I'm also applying to a couple of AI safety fellowships this month. If after Monday you felt able to be a referee, I'd be grateful, but that's a question for after the conversation, not before it.
