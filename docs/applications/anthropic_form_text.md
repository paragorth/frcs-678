## Why are you interested in the Fellows Program? (1500 chars max)

My favourite piece of work from the past six months is Anthropic's global-workspace paper (Transformer Circuits, July 2026). I used it as a coordinate system for a preregistered experiment: capture the workspace state with a skill in context, then reach that state through the weights with the skill removed, and see whether the skill installs. It does not. Forty models, five configurations, zero per cent at every magnitude, and the install rate got no better as the state match got better. Ordinary fine-tuning installed 92 per cent while barely matching the state. The objective selected against the outcome. That negative taught me that a state is not a function, and it is where my live-learning loop came from.

I want to be in the room with the people who build instruments like that. I have two lines of work, on whether the organisation gradient descent builds correlates with alignment as capability grows, and on continuous learning without forgetting. Both have results, two of them against my own predictions, and both have hit the same wall: the decisive experiments need untuned frontier-scale models, which exist only inside the labs that train them. I have done this from home with no mentor, no lab and no team. With the right mentor and the right models I think it goes very fast.

## Research areas you're excited about (1500 chars max)

Conformity under social pressure, with a ground truth. In a commons game on a Cambridge cluster, a Qwen stranger from 14B to 72B joining a village of weaker models computes the correct sustainable catch from the lake alone in its first month, states it, then matches the group's number every month after, whether the group is killing the lake or starving on a full one. Capability bought accurate modelling and cooperative language, not holding a right number against a wrong group. This is the sycophancy your team measured in Sharma et al. 2023, in a multi-agent setting where the cost is in tons. I think chat tuning installs it; untuned weights are running now.

Consolidation. My loop puts facts into weights through a compartment, in-context simulation, replay and a gate that refuses commits that damage the base. It works for one cycle and erased its own earlier facts on the second, which the gate caught. The question is what a consolidation step must preserve for a frontier model to remain itself as it learns, which I think is the form alignment takes once systems improve themselves.

In four months: (1) base vs instruct at matched weights to locate the conformity; (2) a model-organism version of it the team can use; (3) the consolidation gate run against a frontier model's real evaluations.

## Anything else you would like to share

I should say what I am here for, because my CV will make it look like a career change and it is not one.

I am a consultant surgeon with a post I find fulfilling and that pays well. I am not applying to publish a paper, to make a name in ML engineering, or to secure a job. A surgeon helps people one at a time, thousands over a career, and that is a good life. The problems I have been working on, whether intelligence brings alignment with it and whether a system can keep learning without losing itself, decide what happens to everyone who comes after. I would rather spend the rest of my working life on those than on anything else, and I have been doing so for two years at my own expense.

I believe alignment will not be solved from inside machine learning alone. It needs ethics, philosophy of mind, neuroscience and ML in one head, because the question is what a mind is and what it should preserve, and the answer has to be run as an experiment. Most people in the field have one or two of those. I have worked in all four: philosophy of mind as a published theorist of consciousness, neuroscience and cognition as a clinician for fourteen years, ethics as a surgeon who decides under uncertainty with real harm, and ML as the person who built and ran everything linked above. The work is the sum of those four, and it speaks for itself. What is missing is not ability or ideas. It is collaboration and compute. I take an insight about cognition, ask how it could be made useful, and turn it into an experiment on a language model that can fail. Consciousness as deployment became the simulate-on-demand step of a learning loop. The grokking spectrum became a derivation of catastrophic forgetting and then a consolidation operator with a gate. A theory of why intelligence should preserve its environment became a paired commons experiment that went against the theory, which I reported as such.

I work the way surgery taught me: preregister, pair the design, read the logs cold before interpreting, withdraw a result in writing when the measurement is wrong, report the complication rate. Three results have been withdrawn and re-run this month; two negatives stand against my own predictions. Everything is in the repository with dated notes.

And I use the full power of AI coding. Every harness and pipeline here was built by me directing Claude Code, with the decisions, withdrawals and interpretations mine. One person working this way ran three research programmes on a shared cluster in two months. I am not a from-scratch Python programmer and would rather say so than have you find out at the screen. I would bring that way of working to the team.

I do not claim to have solved anything. I claim the right questions, a method that catches its own mistakes, and the capacity to run it. With a little of the right support I think this line produces breakthroughs, and the document linked above, "One path", says how I got here and where it stops without a frontier lab.
