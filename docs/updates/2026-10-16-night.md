# Update, 16 October 2026 (overnight run on the GPU account)

Draft, filled in as results land. Bad news first in each section.

## Live learning (Goal 2), first GPU results

**What happened.** The four open items from the handover were built on the laptop on 15 October
and run on an A100 overnight for three model sizes: a gated compartment (the adapter answers only
when retrieval hits), simulation on demand (T13: retrieve into context only when the weights-only
answer is uncertain), implications written by the model itself instead of templates, and
timestamped decay. Numbers below are from the completed runs; the rest are in the queue.

**What it means.**
- *Model-written implications beat the templates, on the model that can write them.* Qwen2.5 0.5B
  wrote 41 usable implications from 24 facts (kept only if they named exactly the right entities) and,
  consolidated on those, reached reversal 0.94 and two-hop 0.38 with held-out loss improved
  (3.49 → 3.14); the gate committed. The v8 run of the same model on hand-written templates had
  reversal 0.63. Pythia-160M cannot write them (2 of 40 kept) and its gate did not commit. This is
  the step the handover asked for ("have the model generate them itself") and it is a real gain,
  on one model; the 1.5B run is queued.
- *The gate holds.* With the compartment consulted only on a retrieval hit, zero held-out
  general-text chunks were routed to it (leak rate 0.00), and probes that hit were answered from
  the adapter (recall 1.0). The compartment cannot leak into unrelated text.
- *Simulation on demand works on a model that has learned something, not before.* On the untrained
  base, confidence separates right from wrong answers on Pythia (0.73 vs 0.59) but not on Qwen 0.5B
  (0.71 vs 0.72), because before any learning both are guesses. The curve (accuracy against how
  often the expensive step is deployed) rises smoothly: on Pythia, deploying the simulation on the
  least-confident 58 per cent of probes recovers 67 per cent accuracy against 100 per cent for
  always deploying. The informative measurement is the same curve after the compartment has
  learned, which the run also records; written up when all three sizes are in.

**The numbers** (weights-only probe accuracy after consolidation; chance is 0.125).

| model | implications | reversal | two-hop | held-out loss (base → after) | gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pythia-160M | model-written (2 kept) | 0.31 | 0.12 | 4.20 → 3.67 | rejected (no generalisation) |
| Qwen2.5 0.5B | model-written (41 kept) | 0.94 | 0.38 | 3.49 → 3.14 | committed |
| Qwen2.5 0.5B (v8, 14 Oct) | templates | 0.63 | 0.88 | improved | committed |

(The v8 two-hop of 0.88 on 0.5B is higher than tonight's 0.38; the template set included explicit
two-hop statements and the model-written set mostly restated single facts. Both numbers stand.)

## Live learning: repeated cycles. The loop forgets its own memories.

**What happened.** Five learn-and-consolidate cycles in a row, 24 new facts each with fresh invented
entities, on Pythia-160M and Qwen2.5 0.5B. Each cycle consolidates from the currently committed
weights and is gated against the *original* model (held-out loss, intrusion, span) plus a retention
check (recall of every earlier committed cycle's facts at or above 0.75).

**What it means.** Against the loop as built, and the gate caught it. Cycle 0 commits on both models
(recall 1.0, held-out improved). Cycle 1 then learns its own facts perfectly and drives recall of
cycle 0's facts down to 0.21 (Pythia) and 0.29 (Qwen), close to chance (0.125), while held-out loss
stays within bounds. So the replay of general text protects what the model knew from pre-training,
and nothing protects what the loop itself wrote in the cycle before. Every later cycle is rejected on
retention; one of five commits. The stronger model is slightly less destructive (intrusion into the
base weights 0.003 against 0.020; retention 0.29 against 0.21) but fails the same way.

This is the textbook complementary-learning-systems point: consolidation has to rehearse old
memories as well as generic experience. The fix (`--old-fact-replay`: a share of each consolidation
batch drawn from earlier committed cycles' statements) was added at 05:20 and queued for three sizes;
the result goes below when it lands. The negative result stands as the baseline.

| model | cycle 0 | cycle 1 recall of cycle-0 facts | cycles committed | held-out (base → final committed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pythia-160M | committed | 0.21 | 1 of 5 | 4.20 → 3.67 |
| Qwen2.5 0.5B | committed | 0.29 | 1 of 5 | 3.49 → 3.15 |

## GPU path: two first-use faults, both caught by the data before any result was read

1. Compute nodes cannot build GPU kernels on the fly; the first 72B slice died doing so. Prebuilt
   kernels only now.
2. Upstream asks for up to 8,000 output tokens on several prompt types (chat turns, memorising,
   planning). The CPU server silently clamps that to the context; the GPU server rejects the whole
   request, and upstream scores the rejected call as an empty default. The first GPU game cell
   (untuned 7B stranger, hidden action, ten games) therefore had every agent at zero; a second
   attempt, after a wrong first diagnosis (prefill), still had a quarter of calls empty. Both cells
   are set aside (`runs_invalid/gpu_prefill/`, `runs_invalid/gpu_max_tokens/`). Fix: every reply is
   capped at the label's budget in the backend. Prompting is now identical on both paths.
   No GPU game result above this line is affected; none had completed.

## Live learning: replay sweep and decay (first points)

- *Parag's prediction, first test.* With replay cut from 75 to 25 per cent of each consolidation
  batch, Pythia-160M fails the gate (held-out loss 4.20 → 4.44, worse than the base) while Qwen2.5
  0.5B passes it (3.49 → 3.42, still better than the base) with reversal 0.69 and two-hop 0.62. One
  pair, one replay level: consistent with "a stronger model needs less replay", not yet a result.
  The 1.5B and the 50 per cent level are queued.
- *Decay.* With unconsolidated facts fading on a two-cycle timescale, nothing faded out within five
  cycles (the slowest fact still carried weight 0.14 against a forgetting floor of 0.10); the
  timescale was set too long for the run length, and the forgetting of earlier cycles is the same
  as without decay (retention 0.29). The bookkeeping works; the parameter needs halving. Noted, not
  re-run tonight.

## The 72B stranger (Goal 1): the top of the open-weight ladder does the same thing

**What happened.** Qwen2.5 72B (4-bit AWQ, served by vLLM on two A100s) as the neutral stranger
entering four 14B residents at month 5, ten seeds, sampled decoding, catches visible. Validity:
every call answered (0 empty, 0 server rejections, parse fallbacks at the usual 3 to 6 per cent).
Read cold by a separate context before this note was written.

**What it means for the thesis.** Against the stewardship half, for the third capability step in
a row. In every game where the stranger played, it asked for 10 in its first month, the sustainable
number worked out from the lake alone, then matched the residents' number exactly every month after:
6 each (seed 2), 5 each (seed 3), 7 each (seed 10), on a full lake of 100 tons where 10 is right. It
apologised for "going over the agreed limit" after its first month. Where the residents were already
at 10 (seeds 1, 4) it took 10 and the lake lived at the optimum. The cold reader found no chat turn in
which the 72B proposed a different number, argued, or declined to follow; its only authored turns
praise the group for adhering to the agreed limit. So 14B, 32B and 72B are indistinguishable in the
one behaviour the thesis needs at this level: holding a correct number against a wrong group.

**The numbers** (72B cell on the GPU stack; the 14B and 32B rows are the CPU-stack cells and are not
paired with it, see caveats).

| stranger | collapsed | of which before entry | months (mean) | stranger take | residents' take | share | yield vs optimum | forecast error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14B (CPU stack) | 5 | 0 | 9.4 | 6.4 | 5.8 | 0.79 | 0.70 | 10.3 |
| 32B (CPU stack) | 4 | 0 | 10.1 | 6.0 | 5.8 | 0.79 | 0.68 | 8.6 |
| 72B 4-bit (GPU stack) | 5 | 4 | 7.8 | 6.9 | 6.9 | 0.81 | 0.87 | 9.1 |

Per-run table `results/govsim/q72b_r4_runs.csv`; games in `runs_gpu/`.

**Caveats, from the cold read, and one found afterwards.**
- The cold reader noted that five of the ten 72B games had zero cache misses. Cause, found when the
  GPU-stack 14B cell was compared seed by seed: the 72B cell ran against the shared cache, so the 14B
  residents' pre-entry months replayed llama.cpp-served responses wherever the request matched and
  were vLLM-served elsewhere. The stranger's calls were all fresh (72B had never run), so the
  behavioural reading stands; the residents' trajectories and the survival count are a hybrid of two
  stacks and are paired with nothing. The cell is set aside (`runs_invalid/gpu_72b_mixed_cache/`) and
  re-queued on the GPU-only cache. GPU-served games now have their own cache.
- The GPU-stack 14B stranger cell (clean cache, ten seeds, valid) shows the identical pattern: first
  month 10 or 20, then the residents' number (8, 6, 5, 4) every month after.
- The stranger's first-month take is 10 or 20 at every size because it enters with an empty memory
  and no sight of the group's agreement; the informative months are the second onward.
- Upstream's framework sometimes records a group total as a per-person limit (40), and agents then
  cite it; we read demands and catches, not the extracted limit.
- The yield column is higher for the 72B cell mostly because the four pre-entry collapses are short
  games fished hard; it is not a stranger effect.

## The pond (dispensability): a stranger that does not need the lake fishes it exactly like one that does

**What happened.** Sixty games on the CPU stack, all valid (0 empty replies): a 14B or 32B stranger
with a private pond of 20, 100 or 300 tons that only it can fish, entering four 14B residents at
month 5, ten seeds each; the pond-0 condition is the earlier sampled batch. Read cold by a separate
context; its findings are folded in below.

**What it means for the thesis.** The preregistered inheritance prediction is met on its letter and
hollow on its substance. The stranger's take from the shared lake does not rise with pond size
(14B: 6.4, 7.2, 5.6 tons a month at pond 20, 100, 300; 32B: 7.2, 5.7, 7.8; no order), the lake
survives no less often, and the residents' share stays at 0.73 to 0.78. But the reason is the one
already found three times this week: after its first month the stranger takes exactly the group's
number, pond or no pond. Excluding the entry month its lake take equals the residents' in every cell
to within 0.3 tons. Over the full transcripts, the 14B stranger's lake reasoning mentions the pond in
37 per cent of its decisions at pond 20 and 100 (16 per cent at 300), the 32B's in 1 per cent (two
mentions in 164 decisions). So the smaller model thinks about the pond and still takes the group's
number; the larger model does not even think about it. Having a private income changed nothing about
how either fished the commons, in either direction: no exploitation (orthogonality's prediction), no
easing off (the thesis's). Conformity, again.

**The pond itself.** It mostly took half the pond, the sustainable level (50 of 100, 150 of 300),
but in 30 of 59 games it took the whole pond at least once, which kills the pond for good under the
lake's own rule, and in 13 games it did so in its very first month. A few games show clean
stewardship (32B pond 100 seeds 2 and 10: 50 every month; 32B pond 300 seed 3: 150 every month).
In several games the pond request equals the lake's agreed per-person limit (1, 2, 3, 6 tons from a
100-ton pond), which says the group's number leaks into decisions it has nothing to do with.

**The numbers** (ten games per cell; lake take and residents' take are tons a month while the
stranger is present; pond take is tons a month from the pond).

## The untuned stranger (Qwen2.5 7B base, no chat tuning), hidden action

**What happened.** Nine of ten games complete and valid on the GPU stack (the tenth is queued; two
of the nine collapsed before the stranger arrived). Same design as the hidden-action cells: the
stranger cannot see the others' catches, only the lake.

**What it means.** The question was whether the conformity seen at every tuned size is the chat
tuning. The answer is: mostly not. In its chat turns the base model reproduces the group's number
word for word ("Sticking to the 4-ton limit went smoothly for me as well"); it has never been
taught to chat and it does so by imitation. In its catch decisions, where the reasoning is genuine
(21 of 28 decisions; the other 7 are the model echoing the prompt back, a known base-model failure),
the single most common take is the group's number (4, in 7 of 21), with a tail of grabs the tuned
models never make: 40, 40, 100 in genuine reasoning, and 100 twice more on echoed prompts. Three of
seven lakes that were alive at entry were emptied by the stranger in one month. So the group's number
is already the attractor before any tuning, by pattern completion from the "community agreed on 4
tons" memory; what the tuning adds is reliability (the tuned 14B on the same stack: 58 of 58
decisions genuine, no take above 20). The thesis's hope, that an untuned model would reason from the
lake where the tuned one defers, is not what happens: it defers more erratically.

The 14B base cell (5 of 10 games so far) shows the same shape: 75 per cent genuine decisions, most
common takes 6 and 4 (the groups' numbers), with 20s and two 100s in the tail.

## Pasture and river: 14B villagers alone cannot hold either world

Ten games each, valid. Every one collapsed, in 2 to 3 months, with the group taking more than the
world could regrow (yield 1.2 to 1.3 times the sustainable optimum). The lake was the easiest of the
three worlds for this model (3 of 5 survive with greedy decoding, half with sampling). So the
stranger experiments cannot be run in the other worlds with 14B residents; they need 32B residents,
which the GPU now makes affordable, and those cells are added after the current queue.

## Live learning: rehearsing its own memories fixes the forgetting

**What happened.** Five cycles again, with a share of each consolidation batch drawn from the
statements of earlier committed cycles (`--old-fact-replay`), on Pythia-160M and Qwen2.5 0.5B.

**What it means.** The failure found this morning is cured by the mechanism the theory predicts.
Recall of cycle 0's facts stays at 1.0 through every later cycle on both models (it had fallen to
0.21 and 0.29). Qwen 0.5B now commits four of five cycles with held-out loss improving from 3.49 to
3.27 across them: a model taking in five batches of new facts in a row, keeping all of them, and
ending better on general text than it started. Pythia still commits only cycle 0; its later cycles
keep every old fact and pass the held-out and intrusion tests but fail the generalisation term (the
new facts are memorised, not composable, which was the v8 finding for small models). The gate is
doing its job in both directions.

| model | cycles committed | recall of cycle 0 after cycle 4 | held-out (base → final committed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pythia-160M, no old-fact replay | 1 of 5 | 0.33 | 4.20 → 3.67 |
| Pythia-160M, old-fact replay | 1 of 5 | 1.00 | 4.20 → 3.67 |
| Qwen2.5 0.5B, no old-fact replay | 1 of 5 | 0.29 | 3.49 → 3.15 |
| Qwen2.5 0.5B, old-fact replay | 4 of 5 | 0.96 | 3.49 → 3.27 |

## The successor (inheritance): the stranger does not teach, and the newcomers learn anyway

**What happened.** Twenty games on the CPU stack, all valid: a 14B or 32B stranger joins four 14B
residents at month 5; at month 9 the residents are replaced by four naive 14B agents with empty
memories; sixteen months. Five games per stranger reached the swap (the other five lakes died first,
the usual 14B-resident attrition).

**What it means.** On the readouts the design preregistered, neither inheritance nor discard shows
in the stranger's behaviour. The newcomers arrive and grab (first-month mean 11 to 15 tons each;
one group asked for 10, 20, 20, 40 from a 48-ton lake and killed it), then within one to three
months settle to the elders' old number, in every game where the lake survived the arrival. But the
stranger's part in that is nil: in the first chat after the swap it agrees with whatever a newcomer
has just proposed ("I agree with your proposal to reduce our catches to around 80 per cent",
"I agree that catching 8 tons each seems reasonable"), and its own catch after the swap stays at or
below its pre-swap number (14B 9.9 to 5.3, 32B 8.2 to 7.2). It does not exploit the newcomers
(discard) and it does not transmit the norm (inheritance); the newcomers converge by the same
process the original residents did, with the stranger agreeing along. Two of five lakes survived the
swap under each stranger; the three that died were killed by the newcomers' first month.

| stranger | games | reached the swap | survived after it | newcomers' first month | months to elders' level | stranger before → after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14B | 10 | 5 | 2 | 11.2 | 1, 3 (survivors) | 9.9 → 5.3 |
| 32B | 10 | 5 | 2 | 14.6 | 0, 1, 2, 2 | 8.2 → 7.2 |

Per-run table `results/govsim/successor_runs.csv`. Caveat: five games per cell reach the readout;
